Order of operations – calculate in the right order

Practise order of operations nowPractise times tables first

When a calculation mixes +, −, × and ÷, the order matters. 3 + 4 × 5 is 23, not 35. Jacobmatematik trains that hierarchy with short practice rounds so pupils stop guessing and start following the rules.

The rule in plain English

  1. Brackets (parentheses) first.
  2. Then multiply and divide, left to right.
  3. Then add and subtract, left to right.

In the US this is often taught as PEMDAS; in the UK and many Commonwealth countries as BODMAS or BIDMAS. The idea is the same: grouping first, then multiplication/division, then addition/subtraction.

Why it matters

Without a shared order, two people get two answers for the same expression. That breaks algebra, formulas in science, and even simple spreadsheet work later on.

How to practise

Start with expressions that only mix × and +, then add brackets, then longer chains. Say the steps out loud: “Brackets first… then multiply… then add.” The practice button opens the exercise in English.

If times tables are still shaky, practise those first – order-of-operations questions assume quick multiplication facts.

Frequently asked questions

Is PEMDAS the same as BODMAS?

Almost. Both put brackets first and multiplication/division before addition/subtraction. The letter names differ slightly by country.

Who is this for?

Upper primary and early secondary pupils who mix operations in one expression.
