Times tables are the foundation for almost every maths topic that comes later: division, fractions, percentages and equations. When a child can answer 7 × 8 without counting on their fingers, there is room in the head for new ideas. On Jacobmatematik you can practise every table from 1 to 10 for free, in the browser, with no ads.

The small table: 100 multiplication facts

The “small table” is every product from 0 × 0 to 10 × 10. That sounds like a lot, but many facts are easy or repeat. Multiply by 0 and you always get 0; multiply by 1 and you get the same number; multiply by 10 and you add a zero. And 3 × 7 is the same as 7 × 3, so learning one fact unlocks the other. That leaves only about 30 facts that truly need memorising.

How to learn the times tables – step by step

  1. Start with the easy ones. The 1, 2, 5 and 10 times tables have clear patterns. The 2 times table is doubles; the 5 times table always ends in 0 or 5.
  2. Use the commutative rule. If you know 4 × 9, you also know 9 × 4. That halves the work.
  3. Build on what you know. The 4 times table is double the 2 times table; the 8 times table is double again. 9 × 6 is 10 × 6 minus 6.
  4. Practise a little every day. Five to ten minutes a day beats a long Sunday session.
  5. Add speed when accuracy is solid. Once answers are right, the next step is answering quickly so the facts become automatic.

Hard facts and memory tricks

Most children struggle with the same facts: 6 × 7, 6 × 8, 7 × 8 and 8 × 8. A classic trick for 7 × 8 is “5, 6, 7, 8” → 56 = 7 × 8. For the 9 times table, use your fingers: hold ten fingers up, fold down finger number 4 for 9 × 4, and read 3 before and 6 after – 36. The digit sum of answers in the 9 times table is always 9.

From times tables to division

Once the tables stick, you can run them backwards. 63 ÷ 7 means finding the number that times 7 gives 63. Jacobmatematik also has a division drill for the missing factor across the 1–9 table – a good next challenge.

Frequently asked questions

Is it free to practise times tables here?

Yes. All practice on Jacobmatematik is free, and there are no ads.

Do I need an account?

No. You can play as a guest straight away. Create an account to save results; schools can follow a whole class.

Which table should my child start with?

Start with the 1, 2, 5 and 10 times tables. Then move to 3 and 4, and finally 6, 7, 8 and 9.

How long does it take to learn the times tables?

It varies, but with five to ten minutes of practice a day most pupils can learn the small table in a few weeks.
